## Guide Overview

The guide describes behavioral competencies as skills and capabilities that drive success. It is intended to help explore a candidate's suitability for a role using its success profile. Interviewers need not ask every question: they should choose the most relevant prompts and use structured probes to get specific evidence. The guide should be used alongside other information gathered during the recruitment process.

The guide also distinguishes behavioral competencies from **skills**, which it describes as applied or practical knowledge needed for effective technical performance in specific roles, groups of roles, or functions.

## How to Use the Questions

1. Select one relevant question for the competency being explored and probe the answer as needed.
2. If further evidence is needed, follow up with one additional question from that competency.
3. Use structured probes to get supporting evidence when the participant's answer is vague or generalized.
4. Select the behavioral indicators that best represent the information and examples the participant shared.
5. Select the rating that best aligns with your assessment of the participant's responses and enter it on the competency page and interview summary.

### Structured Follow-Up Probes

| Dimension | Follow-up question |
|---|---|
| **Actions** | How did you approach it? |
| **Thinking** | Why did you approach it that way? |
| **Outcome** | What was the result? |
| **Learnings** | What did you take away from this? |
| **Application** | When did you use what you learned in a different situation? |

## Behavioral Competencies

### Ensures Accountability

**Definition:** Holds self and others accountable to meet commitments. Measures and tracks team and individual performance, helps the team learn from success, failure, and feedback, and adheres to and enforces goals, policies, and procedures.

**Interview questions**

1. Describe a time you had to set stretch goals and articulate how success would be measured.
2. Recall a time you made a mistake while working with or leading others and had to make it right.
3. Tell me about a time you had to communicate and manage new responsibilities when there was a significant shift in goals midway through a project.
4. Describe a time you tried to improve the effectiveness of a team or workgroup.
5. Describe a time when a member of a team you were leading made a mistake.

**Positive indicators**

- Follows through on commitments and ensures others do the same.
- Takes personal responsibility for decisions, actions, and failures.
- Ensures that feedback loops are effective.
- Ensures that people or teams determine clear performance standards.
- Establishes processes for monitoring and measuring results.

**Negative indicators**

- Doesn't accept personal responsibility.
- Prefers to share accountability with others.
- Provides inadequate feedback to help others course-correct.
- Doesn't ensure that people or teams see commitments through to completion.
- Doesn't gather information on how things are going.

### Tech Savvy

**Definition:** Anticipates and adopts innovations in business-building digital and technology applications. Ensures the team has adequate resources to invest in technology advancements and training, and deploys new technologies to improve the effectiveness of the group and business.

**Interview questions**

1. Give me an example of a new technology that caused major issues for customers.
2. Tell me about the biggest technological challenge you have ever had to overcome.
3. Describe a time when allocating resources for a new technology worked really well for you.
4. Tell me about a time when you persuaded others about the value of a new technology.
5. Tell me about a time when not picking up a new technology was the right decision.

**Positive indicators**

- Proactively researches new technologies.
- Is comfortable learning and adopting new technologies.
- Provides resources to support technological advancements.
- Applies new and emerging technological options that enhance team or organizational outcomes.
- Educates others on the selection and best use of new technologies.

**Negative indicators**

- Prefers to stick with past, familiar technologies.
- Is slow to adopt new technologies.
- Doesn't promote the benefits of learning and adopting new technologies.
- Withholds resources for key technology tools.
- Does not prioritize technology that will improve business results.

### Plans and Aligns

**Definition:** Plans and prioritizes work to meet commitments aligned with organizational goals. Outlines clear plans that put actions in a logical sequence, conveys time frames, aligns work with relevant workgroups, and takes steps to reduce bottlenecks and speed up work.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about a time when you planned and organized a task or project from start to finish.
2. Tell me about a time when you divided a plan into phases and detailed tasks to organize and accomplish the objectives.
3. Describe a time you were creating a timeline and needed to plan for uncertainties or problems.
4. Describe a time you planned your activities to align with others' work on the team.
5. Tell me about a time you had to change plans during a project or task to prioritize something unexpected.

**Positive indicators**

- Aligns work with team and organizational efforts.
- Clarifies and prioritizes critical tasks and resources.
- Balances the need for planning with the need for action.
- Develops contingency plans.
- Uses timelines and identifies proper sequencing of steps.

**Negative indicators**

- Does not align work with the team or others' activities.
- Reacts to immediate needs and neglects priorities.
- Fails to estimate resources.
- Doesn't have contingency plans in place.
- Doesn't carry out tasks in the right sequence.

### Collaborates

**Definition:** Builds partnerships and works collaboratively with others to meet shared objectives. Readily involves others to accomplish goals, stays in touch and shares information, discourages “us versus them” thinking, and shows appreciation for others' ideas and input.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about a successful experience you've had working with people from different teams or functions.
2. Tell me about a time when you built strong relationships where none previously existed.
3. Describe a time when a person or group did not get their share of credit.
4. Describe a time you worked with multiple groups or people who had different interests.
5. Tell me about a time you succeeded in a task or project by collaborating with others.

**Positive indicators**

- Cooperates with others to achieve shared objectives.
- Considers others' interests as well as their own.
- Partners with others to get work done.
- Gives credit for accomplishments.
- Is trusted by others.

**Negative indicators**

- Misses opportunities to collaborate.
- Puts own interests before others'.
- Doesn't encourage communication between groups.
- Doesn't give credit where it's due.
- Prefers to work alone rather than with others.

### Manages Complexity

**Definition:** Makes sense of complex, high-quantity, and sometimes contradictory information to solve problems effectively. Looks at complex issues from multiple angles, explores issues to uncover underlying causes, and sees the main consequences and implications of different options.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about a time you used a process or procedure to solve a problem.
2. Tell me about a time when your chosen solution didn't work and you had to rethink the problem from scratch.
3. Describe a time you were faced with a complex problem and had to get to its essence in a short time period.
4. Tell me about a time you incorporated information from multiple angles to get the full picture of a problem.
5. Describe a time you faced conflicting data about a problem and weren't sure how to approach it.

**Positive indicators**

- Clearly defines the problem before acting.
- Can see root causes.
- Explores multiple sources for information.
- Understands what is relevant or significant and what is not.
- Can evaluate the consequences of different options.

**Negative indicators**

- Sees things as more simple than they are.
- Doesn't take time to define the essence of the problem.
- Draws on limited sources of information.
- Allows intuition to override the facts.
- Doesn't apply systematic problem-solving methods.

### Optimizes Work Processes

**Definition:** Knows the most effective and efficient processes to get things done, with a focus on continuous improvement. Uses metrics and benchmarks to monitor accuracy and quality, makes methods productive and efficient, and promptly addresses process breakdowns.

**Interview questions**

1. Describe a time when you found yourself caught in an inefficient process and had to figure out a better way.
2. Tell me about a time you critiqued an existing workflow process and then learned that the reason for the standard process was sound.
3. Describe a time you helped revise a process for virtual or remote employees.
4. Tell me about a time when your attempt at improving quality worked out really well.
5. Describe the impact of a work or process improvement you helped design or implement.

**Positive indicators**

- Uses defined standards, procedures, and processes to get results.
- Focuses on continuously improving processes.
- Analyzes and addresses process breakdowns.
- Considers the whole system.
- Improves efficiencies and reduces waste in own work.

**Negative indicators**

- Lacks compliance with defined procedures.
- Struggles to figure out effective processes to get things done.
- Pays little attention to process improvement.
- Doesn't capitalize on opportunities for synergy and efficiency.
- Looks at processes in isolation rather than as a full system.

### Cultivates Innovation

**Definition:** Creates new and better ways for the organization to be successful. Approaches professional work in unique or innovative ways, offers original ideas, appreciates others' creative ideas and solutions, and provides helpful input to enhance them.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about the most unusual idea you've had that worked.
2. Describe a time when you helped size up an idea for its value in the market or organization.
3. Tell me about a time when you came up with some new ideas to solve an old problem.
4. Tell me about a time you helped improve an idea or solution offered by a colleague.
5. Tell me about a time you took a risk on a new product or solution and it paid off.

**Positive indicators**

- Generates ideas that are new, different, and better.
- Looks at problems in a new way.
- Puts creative ideas into practice.
- Builds on the ideas and solutions others offer.
- Considers the value or market potential of new ideas.

**Negative indicators**

- Doesn't experiment with ideas; stays in comfort zone.
- Doesn't embrace others' original ideas.
- Is narrow, conservative, and cautious.
- Stifles creativity in others.
- Doesn't see the value or potential of new ideas.

### Communicates Effectively

**Definition:** Develops and delivers multi-mode communications that convey a clear understanding of the unique needs of different audiences. Listens attentively and takes an interest, keeps others well informed, and conveys information clearly, concisely, and professionally when speaking or writing.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about a time when you had to communicate something important to someone who did not understand your industry's or function's language very well.
2. Describe the best presentation you've ever given.
3. Tell me about a time when others were missing the key points in a discussion and you helped get things back on track.
4. Tell me about a time during a meeting when hearing someone else's opinion altered your view of the situation.
5. Describe a time you had to convey a message both thoroughly and concisely.

**Positive indicators**

- Is effective with a variety of communication methods.
- Listens attentively and actively.
- Adapts approach to the audience.
- Provides timely, helpful information.
- Openly expresses ideas and opinions.

**Negative indicators**

- Is unclear in written and verbal communication.
- Doesn't adjust approach to suit the audience.
- Doesn't listen well to others' viewpoints.
- Doesn't share information others need.
- Is not concise when communicating.

### Nimble Learning

**Definition:** Learns through experimentation when tackling new problems, using both successes and failures as learning fodder. Experiments to find the best possible solution, gains insight from test cases, makes use of new concepts and principles when addressing problems, and learns from mistakes to avoid repeating them.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about a time you did something in a different way following reflection on a previous experience.
2. Tell me about a time you analyzed a mistake or failure in order to promote team learning.
3. Describe an experimental approach you took to solve a problem.
4. Tell me about a time when you took a risk and quickly applied what you learned.
5. Describe a time when you convinced colleagues to keep experimenting until a viable solution was found.

**Positive indicators**

- Analyzes successes and failures.
- Likes a new challenge.
- Learns from mistakes and adjusts accordingly.
- Has a questioning, experimental approach.
- Gets to the essence and extracts learning.

**Negative indicators**

- Stays in comfort zone.
- Gives up too soon.
- Doesn't incorporate lessons learned from success or failure.
- Doesn't use a variety of approaches.
- Doesn't experiment or test solutions.

### Courage

**Definition:** Steps up to address difficult issues, saying what needs to be said. Shares ideas and points of view openly regardless of potential criticism or risk, shows conviction when faced with adversity and challenges, and raises difficult topics to ensure they are addressed.

**Interview questions**

1. Tell me about a time when someone more senior confronted you about something you had done.
2. Describe a time when you challenged an idea or approach.
3. Tell me about a time when you presented a controversial opinion to higher management.
4. Tell me about a time when you stepped in to defend someone whom you felt was being unfairly treated.
5. Tell me about a time you accepted an assignment where the odds of success were against you.

**Positive indicators**

- Able to take the heat.
- Faces up to problems and challenges.
- Takes on the unknown.
- Delivers difficult messages.
- Follows convictions in the face of adversity.

**Negative indicators**

- Not prepared to take a stand on important issues.
- Shies away from challenging situations.
- Is uncomfortable with uncharted territory.
- Avoids conflict or criticism.
- Finds it difficult to express views directly.

### Decision Quality

**Definition:** Makes good and timely decisions that keep the organization moving forward. Knows when to act independently and when to escalate issues, integrates various inputs, decision criteria, and trade-offs, and typically makes good independent decisions.

**Interview questions**

1. Describe a time when you made a project-related decision and were really pleased with the outcome.
2. Tell me about a quick decision you made that turned out to be a good one.
3. Describe a time you had to make a recommendation and gather a lot of information in a short time frame.
4. Give me an example of using good judgment in determining whether to make a decision on your own or to escalate it.
5. Describe a time you received useful feedback on a decision you made.

**Positive indicators**

- Objectively gathers and analyzes information.
- Makes timely decisions.
- Considers criteria and trade-offs to make effective decisions.
- Complies with policies and standards.
- Knows when to decide independently or gather others' input.

**Negative indicators**

- Makes quick decisions without needed analysis.
- Lacks confidence to make routine, timely, or independent decisions.
- Relies too much on self when others' input is needed.
- Lets feelings play too significant a role.
- Is not orderly in thinking.

### Business Insight

**Definition:** Applies knowledge of business and the marketplace to advance the organization's goals. Shows business insight beyond the fundamentals, asks probing questions, and draws on a variety of sources to gain insight into business drivers or industry trends.

**Interview questions**

1. What are three trends in your current industry?
2. Tell me about a successful business and why you think it is successful.
3. Tell me about three advantages that your most recent organization's competitors have.
4. Tell me about a failing business and why you think it is struggling.
5. Tell me about a time your knowledge of marketplace trends influenced a decision you made.

**Positive indicators**

- Understands how businesses work.
- Understands the relationship between different aspects of business.
- Is aware of current business trends.
- Applies business knowledge to the job.
- Understands the competition.

**Negative indicators**

- Doesn't understand how businesses work.
- Is not interested in broad business issues or trends.
- Doesn't understand the relationship between different business elements.
- Doesn't apply business knowledge and information.
- Is too internally focused on only their organization.

## Interview Summary and Ratings

The guide's summary page records the interviewer and date, then provides a rating for each of the 12 behavioral competencies. It advises keeping summary ratings consistent with the individual competency-page ratings.

**Rating scale**

| Rating | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Not at all developed |
| 2 | Underdeveloped |
| 3 | Competent |
| 4 | Very strong |
| 5 | Outstanding |
| NE | No evidence |

**Interviewer recommendation options**

- Do not progress
- Progress to next stage
- Recommend for role

The summary also includes a field for additional comments in support of the recommendation. The guide has a notes page for capturing additional interview notes. On competency pages, the interviewer selects behavioral indicators that best reflect the participant's examples and enters a rating.
